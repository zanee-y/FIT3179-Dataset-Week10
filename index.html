Tracking Malaysia's Water Balance

Most Malaysian states maintain a healthy water balance where

water management practices However some states show concerning

Where Is Water Production Highest?

Selangor records the highest water production capacity followed
closely by Johor and Perak These states have
the infrastructure to support their larger populations and
industrial activities requiring substantial water resources for both
domestic and non-domestic usage across various sectors

Smaller states like Perlis and W P Labuan maintain
more modest production levels reflecting their population sizes
and development patterns Their water infrastructure is scaled appropriately
to meet local demands without significant excess capacity

These results highlight Malaysia's strategic water management approach
where production capacity aligns with regional needs and development
priorities ensuring sustainable water resources for future growth

Key Insights:
• Selangor: Highest production, supports largest population
• Johor: Second highest, industrial and residential demand
• Perak: Strong production for agricultural and urban needs
• W.P. Labuan: Lowest production, smallest population

Urban-Rural Water Access Equity

Bridging Malaysia's Water Access Gap

Urban areas achieve near universal water access while rural
communities face significant gaps with Kelantan and Sabah
showing the largest disparities Infrastructure challenges particularly affect
remote areas though substantial progress has been made
over the past decade Continued investment gradually narrows
access differences between urban and rural populations Federal
territories such as W P Labuan demonstrate that full
access is achievable serving as important development benchmarks
Melaka and W P Labuan show remarkable consistency with
zero gap differences maintained annually Water equity remains
a key priority for national development and social welfare
