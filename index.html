Malaysia Water Management Analysis

Water consumption, production and access across states (2012-2022)

Geographic Distribution of Water Consumption

Per Capita Water Consumption by State and Sector (2012-2022)

Water consumption varies significantly across Malaysian states with urbanized

areas like Selangor showing highest demand while rural states

have much lower usage levels. Domestic consumption spreads evenly across

regions while non-domestic usage concentrates in industrial hubs, with

population density driving overall consumption patterns. It can be seen

that Selangor has the most water consumption in Malaysia due

to its dense population which requires heavy daily water usage.

Water Production vs Consumption Trends

Water Production vs Consumption Trends by State (2012-2022)

Most Malaysian states maintain a healthy water balance where production

generally exceeds consumption levels, indicating sustainable water management practices.

However, some states show consumption approaching production, signaling potential future

water stress as rapid development increases water demand. It is

obvious that Selangor has the highest and W.P. Labuan has

the lowest water production and consumption.

Water Production Distribution

Annual Water Production Distribution by State

Water production capacity varies greatly across Malaysian states with Selangor

and Johor leading in production to support their larger populations,

while smaller states maintain more modest capacities. The circular radial

format allows for quick visual comparison between states, revealing production

patterns that might be less obvious in traditional charts. Production

growth generally matches regional development patterns, with infrastructure investment

being the primary driver of capacity increases over time.

Urban-Rural Water Access Equity

Urban-Rural Water Access Difference by State

While urban areas achieve near universal water access, rural communities

face significant gaps with Kelantan and Sabah showing the largest disparities.

Infrastructure challenges particularly affect remote areas, though substantial progress has

been made over the past decade with continued investment gradually

narrowing access differences. Federal territories such as W.P. Labuan demonstrate

that full access is achievable, serving as important development benchmarks

for other regions working toward water equity. Do highlight that

Melaka and W.P. Labuan have zero gap differences due

to their water access every year being consistent.
