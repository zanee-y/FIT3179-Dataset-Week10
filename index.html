Water Consumption Patterns

Water consumption varies significantly across Malaysian states with urbanized areas like Selangor showing highest demand while rural states have much lower usage levels. Domestic consumption spreads evenly across regions while non-domestic usage concentrates in industrial hubs, with population density driving overall consumption patterns. Production-Consumption Balance

Most Malaysian states maintain a healthy water balance where production generally exceeds consumption levels, indicating sustainable water management practices. However, some states show consumption approaching production, signaling potential future water stress as rapid development increases water demand. Water Production Capacity

Water production capacity varies greatly across Malaysian states with Selangor and Johor leading in production to support their larger populations, while smaller states maintain more modest capacities. The circular radial format allows for quick visual comparison between states, revealing production patterns that might be less obvious in traditional charts. Production growth generally matches regional development patterns, with infrastructure investment being the primary driver of capacity increases over time.

Urban-Rural Water Access Equity

Urban-Rural Water Access Difference

Urban-Rural Water Access

While urban areas achieve near universal water access, rural communities face significant gaps with Kelantan and Sabah showing the largest disparities. Infrastructure challenges particularly affect remote areas, though substantial progress has been made over the past decade with continued investment gradually narrowing access differences. Federal territories demonstrate that full access is achievable, serving as important development benchmarks for other regions working toward water equity.
